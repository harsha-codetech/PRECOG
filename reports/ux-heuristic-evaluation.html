1. Method

Expert heuristic evaluation is a well-validated discount usability method (Nielsen & Molich, 1990; Nielsen, 1994). A single expert evaluator inspects each screen of the prototype against a structured checklist of usability principles, rating each violation on a 0–4 severity scale. Studies show a single expert finds approximately 35% of usability problems; the method is most appropriate in early formative stages before high-fidelity implementation.

The PRECOG prototype was evaluated across all 8 defined screens (WF-01 to WF-08). Each screen was inspected three times: first for layout and visual hierarchy, second for language and comprehension, third for interaction flow and error prevention. All 10 heuristics were applied to each screen.

No catastrophic (severity 4) issues were found. The two major issues both relate to the tolerance band visualisation — the central affordance of the app — and must be resolved before moving to a high-fidelity Figma implementation. The minor issues are concentrated in the Learning Mode and Not Enough Pattern screens, which are the most novel states with no prior-art design patterns to draw from.

3. Per-Screen Findings

WF-01 — Home: Within Range 2 findings
Tolerance band is a genuinely novel visualisation that communicates deviation without a number. The state label, headline, and sub-copy form a clear 3-level information hierarchy.
Confidence shown as "High / Medium / Low" (not a fake decimal) correctly avoids false precision. Aligns with H2 (real world match) and H5 (error prevention).
F-01 · WF-01 · H2 · Sev 2
Tolerance band has no axis labels or scale — users cannot tell what the x-axis represents
H2 — Real world match H6 — Recognition
The band shows "slower ← → faster" but gives no indication of the unit (σ, %, time?), the band width relative to the user's actual variance, or where the population average sits. First-time users cannot anchor the marker to anything meaningful.
Add a faint centre-line labelled "your usual" and a tooltip or footnote: "Band = your normal range. Marker = this session." No numbers needed — the words are enough.
F-02 · WF-01 · H1 · Sev 1
Confidence bar percentage (78%) is visible but not explained
H1 — System status H10 — Help
The bar shows "78% · 840 gestures" but the home screen provides no path to understand what 78% means. A user who notices the bar and wonders "78% of what?" has no way to find out without tapping into session detail.
Make the confidence row tappable, linking to a one-screen explainer: "Confidence is based on how many of your scrolling sessions PRECOG has observed. More sessions = tighter band."
WF-02 — Home: Unusual Alert 1 major finding
The two-button response ("All good" / "Take a break") correctly gives the user agency over the alert rather than forcing an action. Directly addresses the false-positive anxiety concern from the usability testing plan.
Language "outside your normal range" rather than "you're addicted" is correctly non-judgmental and aligns with H2.
F-03 · WF-02 · H4 · Sev 3 — MAJOR
"All good" button has no confirmation or consequence — user cannot tell if tapping it does anything
H4 — Consistency H1 — System status H3 — User control
Tapping "All good" presumably dismisses the alert and logs a false-positive signal, but the prototype gives no feedback — the screen does not change. Users cannot tell if the tap registered, if the alert is dismissed, or if it will return. This is the primary interaction on the most important screen.
After "All good" tap: show a brief confirmation ("Got it — noted as your normal") and transition the state card back to the Within Range state. Log the dismissal for the adaptive threshold. Add this state transition to the hi-fi prototype.
F-04 · WF-02 · H8 · Sev 1
Alert card has no timestamp — user cannot tell if this is the current session or a past one
H1 — System status H8 — Minimalist design
The state card says "Unusual" and shows the tolerance band but does not show which session triggered it or when it started.
Add "Now · Instagram · started 11:14 PM" as a small sub-label under the state tag. Already present in WF-07 Insight Detail — carry it up to the home card.
WF-03 — Home: Engaged State 2 findings
The "Why no alert?" section is the strongest design decision in the prototype. It proactively explains a non-event, which directly addresses the competitive gap vs. Screen Time and Digital Wellbeing. Correct application of H1 (system tells you what it decided and why).
Showing both duration (68 min) and passive ratio (9%) with their baselines makes the reasoning transparent and auditable. Correct application of H6 (recognition).
F-05 · WF-03 · H2 · Sev 2
"Engaged" label may be misread as positive endorsement rather than a neutral state name
H2 — Real world match H4 — Consistency
In everyday language "engaged" carries a positive connotation (engaged at work = productive). A user seeing this state might think the app is praising their session, rather than merely classifying it as intentional-but-long. This risks reinforcing long sessions.
Consider "Intentional" or "Active use" as alternative labels. If "Engaged" is retained (it is the SPEC term), add a micro-label: "Long session — intentional use detected" rather than just the tag.
F-06 · WF-03 · H7 · Sev 1
No way for the user to override the Engaged classification if it is wrong
H3 — User control H7 — Flexibility
If the model incorrectly classifies a compulsive session as Engaged (high passive ratio miscalculated), the user cannot say "actually I do want an alert." There is no override path.
Add a subtle "I want a reminder" link at the bottom of the Engaged card. This gives power users an escape hatch without cluttering the primary view.
WF-04 — Session Detail 2 findings
Deviation bars in σ units with colour-encoding (green within / amber unusual / red pronounced) create a scannble evidence summary. The "evidence-required" design constraint is correctly implemented here.
F-07 · WF-04 · H2 · Sev 3 — MAJOR
σ (standard deviation) units are not meaningful to a general audience
H2 — Real world match H6 — Recognition
Showing "−2.1σ" for inter-scroll interval is technically correct and appropriate for a research paper, but a typical 18–25 year old phone user does not know what a standard deviation is. The deviation bars are visually clear but the label "−2.1σ" will be meaningless to most users, undermining the evidence-required goal.
Replace σ labels with plain language multipliers in the primary view: "2.1× faster than usual" instead of "−2.1σ". Keep σ in a collapsible "technical details" section for power users and for the research paper figures. Both can coexist.
F-08 · WF-04 · H4 · Sev 2
Feature names ("inter-scroll ms", "passive ratio") are technical jargon
H2 — Real world match H4 — Consistency
"inter-scroll ms" and "mean resultant length" are feature names from the Touchalytics literature, not user-facing language. A user cannot act on information they cannot understand.
Map each feature to a plain-language label for display: "inter-scroll ms" → "Pause between scrolls", "passive ratio" → "Passive scrolling", "direction reversals" → "Back-and-forth". Keep technical names in tooltips or a developer mode.
WF-05 — Learning Mode 3 findings
Showing gesture count (27/80) instead of "day 1 of 5" correctly reflects the underlying metric. The copy "not a population average — PRECOG learns your rhythm" is precisely the right differentiation message.
F-09 · WF-05 · H1 · Sev 2
Users do not know what a "gesture" is — progress bar unit is opaque
H2 — Real world match H1 — System status
"27 gestures observed / 80 needed" — users understand sessions and minutes, not gestures. The progress bar is clear visually but its unit is invisible.
Add a translation: "27 scroll swipes observed (about 1 session's worth). Need around 2 more sessions." Show both the technical count and the human translation.
F-10 · WF-05 · H3 · Sev 2
No way to see what PRECOG has already observed during learning — black box feeling
H1 — Visibility H3 — User control
The learning screen shows 3 metric tiles (27, 1, —) but gives no preview of the emerging baseline. The user cannot see whether what PRECOG is learning is accurate, creating a trust gap.
Show a preview: "So far, your median scroll pace is 380ms — we'll use more sessions to make this precise." Even a rough early estimate builds trust in the learning process.
F-11 · WF-05 · H5 · Sev 1
No option to reset or restart the baseline during learning
H3 — User control H5 — Error prevention
If a user installs PRECOG during an exam period (atypically high phone use) and then returns to normal use, their baseline will be permanently skewed. There is no way to discard the current learning data and restart.
Add a "Reset baseline" option in Settings (not visible here, but must exist in hi-fi). During learning, show a faint "Start over" link.
WF-06 — Not Enough Pattern 2 findings
The "Honesty, not a bug" line is the strongest copy in the entire prototype. It reframes system uncertainty as a design virtue rather than a failure — exactly the right tone for the Arjun persona.
Showing the user's spread (±2.8σ) vs. population (±1.1σ) personalises the uncertainty and avoids the implication that the user is doing something wrong.
F-12 · WF-06 · H2 · Sev 2
±2.8σ and ±1.1σ are incomprehensible to a general user
H2 — Real world match
The variability stats use σ units — the same problem as F-07 but in a more critical screen where the user needs reassurance, not confusion. Seeing "±2.8σ" when uncertain about what the app is doing will increase anxiety.
Replace with: "Your scrolling varies a lot day to day — that makes it harder to spot what's unusual for you. ~12 more sessions will help PRECOG calibrate." Drop the numbers entirely on this screen.
F-13 · WF-06 · H1 · Sev 1
The "⋯" icon is ambiguous — could mean loading, or could be decorative
H4 — Consistency H6 — Recognition
The ellipsis "⋯" in the centre of the Not Enough Pattern screen is intended as an honest-uncertainty icon but could be misread as a loading spinner or as a "more options" button.
Replace with a waveform or soft shimmer illustration that visually suggests "fuzzy/uncertain" rather than "loading." Or use a question-mark motif with low contrast to suggest incompleteness without alarm.
WF-07 — Insight Detail 2 findings
Feature deviation bars ranked most-deviant-first implement the evidence-required constraint correctly. The confidence section at the bottom grounds the entire card.
The timestamp + app + duration header ("Tuesday 11:14 PM · Instagram · 38 min") provides complete context without taking up primary space. Strong information hierarchy.
F-14 · WF-07 · H3 · Sev 1
No action available from Insight Detail — screen is read-only with no next step
H3 — User control H7 — Flexibility
The insight card shows a deviation and its evidence, but gives the user nothing to do with that information. There is no "mark as expected," no "set a reminder," no "share," no "see similar sessions."
Add two actions at the bottom: "This was normal for me" (false-positive signal) and "Set a reminder next time" (adaptive intervention). Even one action turns a read-only screen into a useful interaction.
F-15 · WF-07 · H8 · Sev 0
Confidence bar at 82% creates false precision alongside plain-language label
H8 — Minimalist design
The confidence section shows both the bar (82%) and the text "high confidence comparison" — the number and the word say the same thing. Minor redundancy.
Remove the percentage from the body copy; keep it as a tooltip only. Let the bar and "high / medium / low" label do the work.
WF-08 — History 2 findings
Colour-coded state chips (Within Range / Unusual / Engaged) across the history list create a visual timeline — one week of chips tells a behavioural story at a glance. Good application of H6 (recognition over recall).
F-16 · WF-08 · H7 · Sev 2
No filtering or search — history list will become unusable after 4–6 weeks of use
H7 — Flexibility and efficiency
The history screen shows a flat chronological list with no way to filter by app, by state, or by date range. After a month of daily use (30+ entries), finding a specific session will require extensive scrolling.
Add a filter bar: "All · Unusual only · By app." Three chips at the top of the list, no additional screen needed. Implement in hi-fi.
F-17 · WF-08 · H1 · Sev 1
No weekly summary — user cannot see overall trend without reading every row
H1 — Visibility H6 — Recognition
Each session is listed individually, but there is no "this week: 2 unusual, 1 engaged, 8 normal" summary. The user has to do their own counting.
Add a collapsed weekly summary chip at the top of each day group: "This week — 2 unusual · 11 normal." Expands to the session list below. Low implementation cost, high cognitive value.
Cross-screen — Consistency & Navigation 1 finding
F-18 · Cross-screen · H4 · Sev 1
Tab bar icon set uses symbols (⌂ ◷ ◈ ⚙) that have no standard meaning on Android
H4 — Consistency and standards
The tab bar uses Unicode symbols rather than standard Material Design icons. Android users expect the home, clock, person, and settings icons from the Material icon set. Non-standard icons increase learning time.
Replace with Material Icons in the Figma hi-fi: home, history (clock), insights (bar_chart), settings. All free and platform-standard.

6. Design Strengths

  • Tolerance band visualisation — a novel, non-numerical way to communicate personal deviation. No prior digital-wellbeing app uses this pattern.
  • ENGAGED state with explanation — proactively explains a non-alert. Directly solves the false-positive problem that Screen Time and Digital Wellbeing do not address.
  • Evidence-required constraint — every score carries ranked feature deviations. Session Detail and Insight Detail both implement this correctly.
  • Honest uncertainty — "Not Enough Pattern" and the confidence band are the most distinctive design decisions vs. competitors. "Honesty, not a bug" is a standout copy line.
  • Non-judgmental language — "outside your range" not "you're addicted." Consistently maintained across all 5 states.
  • Colour system — green/amber/red state encoding is consistent, accessible, and meaningful without being alarming.

7. Conclusions and Next Steps

The PRECOG prototype demonstrates a clear and original design direction with no catastrophic usability failures. The two major issues (F-03 and F-07) are both fixable with low implementation effort and must be resolved before the Figma hi-fi stage. The dominant theme across violations is technical language leaking into user-facing copy — σ units, feature names, and gesture counts that are correct for the research paper but wrong for a general user.

The prototype is ready to progress to hi-fi Figma with the 2 major and 7 minor fixes applied. A follow-up usability study with 5 real participants (formative) is recommended before any implementation work begins, to validate whether the tolerance band redesign (F-01) achieves comprehension without prompting.

Immediate actions before hi-fi

  • Replace all σ labels with "×faster/slower than usual" phrasing (F-07, F-12)
  • Add "All good" confirmation + state transition (F-03)
  • Add "your usual" anchor to tolerance band (F-01)
  • Map all technical feature names to plain-language equivalents (F-08)
  • Translate "gestures" to "scroll swipes / sessions" in Learning Mode (F-09)
